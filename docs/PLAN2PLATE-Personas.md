# PLAN2PLATE – Personas

## What’s the Business?

First off, let’s define what this business is about.

We want to bring an easier way to grocery shop and plan meals. To achieve our goal, we are collaborating with **Mercator** and opening a new grocery shopping platform **“Plate2Plan”**.

Our webstore will allow users to purchase groceries through set meals they can add to their meal plan.

---

## What is the positioning statement?

For busy professionals, students, and health-conscious planners who struggle with deciding what to cook and want a more efficient way to plan meals and shop groceries.

**Plan2Plate** is a digital meal-planning and grocery integration platform that simplifies weekly food decisions and automatically connects recipes to real-time grocery shopping through Mercator.

# 1. Jakob – The Math Teacher

### Screening Questions
- Do you regularly buy groceries online for your household?
- Do you plan your meals in advance?

### Profile

> <img src="./pictures/Persona1.png"
> style="width:1.67778in;height:1.53083in" />Jakob is 29 years old and
> has recently started working full-time as a Math teacher at the High
> School Garibaldi in Koper. His workday runs from 7:00 to 15:00, but as
> a new teacher he often spends additional time at home preparing
> lessons, grading assignments, and organizing materials. His schedule
> is structured, but busy and mentally demanding.

He values precision, efficiency, and clear systems, both in his
classroom and in his personal life. Because of his long workdays, he
prefers planning his week in advance, including his meals. However,
grocery shopping has become a challenge. He no longer has time to visit
multiple stores to compare prices and search for the best deals, as he
used to do.

Jakob wants a simpler solution: one reliable store where he can find
affordable products consistently, without wasting time. Ideally, he
would like a digital platform that allows him to plan his weekly meals
and automatically generate a shopping list based on recipes. If this
system is integrated with Mercator and enables online ordering with home
delivery or pick-up, it would significantly reduce his stress and save
valuable time during the week.

For Jakob, grocery shopping is not something he enjoys, instead it is a
task he wants to complete as efficiently as possible, preferably
integrated into a structured meal-planning routine.

---

**Thinks**
- Grocery shopping shouldn’t take so much mental energy.

**Sees**
- Long queues and crowded stores after work.

**Feels**
- Mentally drained after work, overwhelmed by daily decision-making. Relieved when his
week is structured and prepared in advance.

**Does**
- Grocery shopping every week with occasional midweek “emergency” store visits. Uses
simple recipes or repeats meals to reduce effort.

# 2. Tina – The Psychology Student

### Screening Questions
- Do you regularly cook for yourself?
- Do you struggle with deciding what to cook?

### Profile

> <img src="./pictures/Persona2.png"
> style="width:1.78819in;height:1.53194in" />Tina is 21 years old and
> studies Psychology at University of Primorska, Faculty of Mathematics,
> Natural Sciences and Information Technologies (FAMNIT) in Koper. She
> lives in a shared apartment and manages her own meals and groceries on
> a limited student budget. Her weekly schedule varies - some days are
> filled with lectures, while others are dedicated to studying, group
> projects, or exam preparation.

One of Tina’s biggest everyday struggles is deciding what to cook. She
often opens the fridge without a clear idea, hoping inspiration will
appear. Instead, she feels overwhelmed by random ingredients that don’t
easily combine into a proper meal. She spends a lot of time scrolling
through social media recipes, but the abundance of options makes the
decision even harder. By the time she chooses something, she’s already
tired or too hungry to cook properly.

Because she doesn’t plan ahead, Tina shops in small, frequent trips and
sometimes buys ingredients without a specific meal in mind. This leads
to food waste, repetitive meals, or ordering takeaway during stressful
periods. She wishes grocery shopping and meal decisions were simpler and
less mentally draining.

---

**Thinks**
- What should I cook today? I don’t want to eat the same thing again. Why is deciding what
to cook harder than actually cooking?

**Sees**
- TikTok and Instagram recipes that look easy but require many ingredients. A fridge with
random ingredients that don’t form a proper meal. Recipe websites that overwhelm her
with too many options.

**Feels**
- Unmotivated when hungry and tired. Overwhelmed by too many choices. Relieved when
someone else decides what she’s cooking.

**Does**
- Sometimes skips meals or eats snacks instead of proper meals. Often decides what to cook
the same day. Frequently buys ingredients without a clear plan.

---

### Problem Scenarios

| Problem | Current Alternatives | Your Value Proposition |
|----------|---------------------|------------------------|
| Tina struggles to decide what to cook daily | Scrolls social media for recipes and eats random meals | Quick “What should I cook today?” feature with 3 simple personalized suggestions |
| She feels overwhelmed by too many recipe choices | Uses TikTok/Instagram; gives up | Recommended fast and easy recipes |

# 3. Marina – The Health-Conscious Accountant

### Screening Questions
- Do you plan your meals weekly?
- Do you actively try to eat healthy?

### Profile

> <img src="./pictures/Persona3.png"
> style="width:1.84792in;height:1.53194in" />Marina is 30 years old and
> works full-time as an accountant in Ljubljana. She has a stable
> 8:00–16:00 office schedule and lives with her partner. Outside of
> work, she is physically active — she goes to the gym 3–4 times per
> week and occasionally attends group fitness classes. Maintaining a
> healthy lifestyle is important to her, and she tries to be mindful
> about exercising and nutrition.

Marina loves to meal plan to meet her daily protein intake goals. She
prefers knowing in advance what she will eat during the week, especially
because she wants balanced meals that support her workouts and overall
wellbeing. However, despite her intentions, meal planning often becomes
time-consuming. She spends Sunday evenings searching for recipes,
calculating whether they are healthy enough, checking ingredients, and
writing shopping lists manually.

Even though she is organized, Marina sometimes feels frustrated by how
disconnected everything is – recipes on one site, has to google
nutritional information and then do grocery shopping in a store. She
wants healthy, structured, efficient planning without spending hours
researching.

---

**Thinks**
- I want my meals to support my workouts. Why does healthy eating require so much
planning?

**Sees**
- Separate apps for recipes, calorie tracking, and grocery shopping. Grocery store discounts
that don’t always match her meal plan.

**Feels**
- Motivated to maintain a healthy lifestyle. Disappointed when she skips meal prep and
ends up eating something less nutritious.

**Does**
- Meal plans once per week. Sometimes tracks calories or macros. Chooses products based
on quality and nutritional value, not just price

---

### Problem Scenarios

| Problem | Current Alternatives | Your Value Proposition |
|----------|---------------------|------------------------|
| Meal planning takes too long | Searches recipes manually - uses spreadsheets | Smart weekly healthy meal plan generator tailored to active lifestyle |
| Grocery shopping doesn’t match planned meals efficiently | Writes manual lists; adjusts in-store | Auto-generated shopping cart synced with Mercator inventory |
| Healthy recipes often require too many ingredients | Simplifies recipes herself | Curated balanced recipes with optimized ingredient overlap |
